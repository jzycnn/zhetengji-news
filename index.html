Microsoft will retire its "To Do" apps on June 30, 2027, ending support for its task manager on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Windows, Android, and the web. In its retirement announcement, Microsoft directs work and school account users to Planner. Personal Microsoft accounts cannot access Planner, including those with Microsoft 365 Personal or Family subscriptions, leaving those users to find another task management app entirely. Switching to Planner will not transfer users' existing To Do tasks and lists. Those tasks will remain available within Planner until June 30, 2027, but users will lose access to them there after that date. Microsoft plans to provide an export option before To Do retires, although it has not explained what the export will include or whether other apps will be able to import it. Mac users moving to Planner will also lose the option of a dedicated desktop app. Planner is available through a browser or Microsoft Teams, and Microsoft has no plans to release a native Mac or Windows app to replace To Do. Neither option offers the same ability to manage tasks offline as the existing desktop apps. To Do grew out of "Wunderlist," the popular task manager whose developer, 6Wunderkinder, Microsoft acquired in 2015. Wunderlist's appeal at the time was its combination of simplicity, shared lists, and synced support across platforms. Microsoft introduced To Do in April 2017 as Wunderlist's intended successor, built by the same team. Initially available as a preview for iPhone, Android, Windows, and the web, it added a My Day view that suggested tasks to focus on. Microsoft said it would keep Wunderlist running until its key features were available in the new app. A native Mac app followed in June 2019, bringing offline access, shared lists, flagged Outlook emails, and a compact window for viewing tasks alongside other apps. Wunderlist was discontinued in May 2020, completing the earlier transition to To Do.
