The measure would clarify a disputed area for state regulators and put operators on notice that the state may treat these games as illegal gambling. Draft targets operators, not a settled legal category The committee’s proposal concerns online sweepstakes games in which prizes are awarded by chance. Wyoming already bars online casinos, while online sports wagering and fantasy sports contests are permitted; sweepstakes games occupy less clearly defined ground under current state law. A mobile device showing an online casino-style game. – Photo: Niek Doup / Unsplash That uncertainty has created a jurisdictional loop. When complaints arrive, the Consumer Protection and Antitrust Unit may view the games as gambling and refer them to the Wyoming Gaming Commission, which can send them back because the issue may fall under consumer-protection law. The proposed ban would make the state’s position clearer and, if enacted, expose operators to felony liability. Wyoming’s move is part of a wider state-level debate over how online casino-style products fit within existing rules. The distinction matters for online casino gaming frameworks, where lawmakers and regulators have to determine which products can operate and under what conditions. The committee also sponsored a separate draft updating fingerprinting requirements for certain people regulated by the Gaming Commission. That proposal addresses a different part of the state’s gaming oversight, rather than the legal status of sweepstakes games. Industry attorney objects to the proposed scope Bill Gantz, an attorney for sweepstakes gaming company VGW, argued that the draft could reach too broadly and conflict with Wyoming’s current treatment of sweepstakes tied to purchases of goods or services. Federal law requires such sweepstakes to offer an alternative method of entry, allowing people to participate without buying anything.
