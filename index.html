California resident Michael Chan brought the lawsuit on behalf of himself and a proposed class of consumers who allegedly lost money using DraftKings Predictions. According to the complaint, Chan registered for the platform while in California and “was not informed by DraftKings that Predictions is an unlicensed sportsbook when he wagered and lost money on the app.” The filing argues DraftKings relies on its established sportsbook brand, technology and customer base to market what plaintiffs describe as the functional equivalent of sports betting in states that have not approved online sportsbooks. “DraftKings has found another way in: it deploys Predictions to evade those states’ gambling laws, regulations, and prohibitions, and sells consumers sports bets that are functionally identical to the wagers in its regulated sportsbook,” the complaint states. Plaintiffs seek to represent consumers from Alabama, California, Florida, Georgia, Minnesota, New Mexico, South Carolina and Texas. The lawsuit says thousands of consumers may qualify, with alleged damages exceeding $5 million under the Class Action Fairness Act. DraftKings Prediction strategy faces test with class action lawsuit A major part of the complaint relies on statements attributed to DraftKings executives during earnings calls and investor presentations. The filing also quotes Robins saying the products “serve the same customers in the same live moments and leverage shared underlying infrastructure.” Plaintiffs contend those remarks support their claim that Predictions extends DraftKings’ sportsbook into markets where licensed online betting remains unavailable. The complaint also cites Robins’ statement that “Some of the biggest states in the country like California, Texas, Florida, we were not present in with [online sports betting], and we have Predictions there now.” DraftKings launched the standalone DraftKings Predictions app across 38 states in late 2025 under Commodity Futures Trading Commission oversight, describing it as a platform for trading event contracts rather than placing sportsbook wagers. The company later announced plans to expand by launching a market-making business and, in May 2026, advanced its strategy after Railbird Exchange submitted sports event contract certifications to the CFTC, including templates resembling moneyline, point-spread and player performance markets. The complaint says DraftKings has been unjustly enriched through an unlicensed sportsbook and, for California consumers, violated the state’s Unfair Competition Law. It states that “substance use disorders and gambling disorders are among the most serious public health problems in Illinois” and says prevention, intervention, treatment and recovery support services should be available to improve the health and well-being of affected individuals, families and communities. Illinois expands behavioral health system to include gambling disorder Another key change is the creation of a formal legal definition for gambling disorder. The statute describes it as “persistent and recurrent problematic gambling behavior leading to clinically significant impairment or distress” that is consistent with the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM-5) published by the American Psychiatric Association. The legislation also gives the Illinois Department of Human Services a wider role. The department will oversee programmes focused on gambling disorder prevention, treatment and recovery support. Its responsibilities include public education campaigns, maintaining a problem gambling website and helpline, supporting research into gambling-related harms, encouraging suicide prevention efforts and awarding grants for gambling disorder services when funding is available. It must also work with the Illinois State Board of Education, where instructional resources are developed, to help make gambling disorder education available in schools. The law also weaves gambling disorder into Illinois’ existing behavioral health system. Licensed substance use disorder treatment providers can offer gambling disorder treatment as an additional service, while recovery residences may accommodate people recovering from gambling disorder. Existing patient rights and confidentiality protections under the Substance Use Disorder Act now extend to those receiving gambling disorder prevention, treatment and recovery services. Senate Bill 2749 was sponsored by Sen. Julie Morrison in the Senate and Rep. Bob Morgan in the House before winning bipartisan approval in the General Assembly. With the governor’s signature, Illinois joins a growing list of states formally recognizing gambling disorder within public behavioral health systems. The move follows wider debate over gambling policy in Illinois. Last year, DraftKings chief executive Jason Robins criticized the state’s planned per-bet tax on major operators to curb problem gambling, arguing during a CNBC interview that higher costs could push consumers towards illegal betting markets or encourage larger wagers. He also warned that operators might have little choice but to pass some of those costs on to customers.
