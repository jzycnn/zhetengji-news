The distinction between event contracts and conventional wagers is central to the CFTC’s approach. Its proposed framework for event contracts is still under consideration, while the gambling exclusion takes effect when it is published in the Federal Register. The justices have not agreed to hear the case, and filing the petition does not lift the injunction or require Kalshi to stop offering its contracts in the state. The continuing uncertainty reflects a broader legal dispute over state gambling rules and federal oversight. For Kalshi’s New Jersey operation, the practical position remains unchanged for now: sports contracts are available under court-ordered temporary protection, not a final ruling that resolves the state’s authority. State safeguards are part of the dispute New Jersey’s petition points to rules it wants to preserve, including a minimum betting age of 21, voluntary self-exclusion and limits on wagers by people with inside information. Licensed sportsbooks in the state also cannot take bets on college events held in New Jersey or involving a New Jersey college team, including Rutgers games played elsewhere.
