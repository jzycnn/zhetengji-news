That beat the $227.8 million won by Atlantic City’s nine casino hotels. The online figure trailed two earlier months this year. Only July, at $276.9 million, and May, at $276.3 million, came in higher. July rose 12.0% and May rose 11.9%. The result reversed the order seen a month earlier. Atlantic City floors stumble while online growth holds up The slump on the casino floors came after a stronger summer. Stockton University gaming analyst Brian Tyrrell said the land-based casinos went from a strong May-to-July run to “one of the worst Septembers in recent history.” Online play moved the other way. The year-on-year comparison is like-for-like on tax. New Jersey raised its gross revenue tax rate on internet gaming from 15% to 19.75% on July 1, 2025. Last September was therefore already taxed at the higher rate. The state collected $82.7 million in gross revenue taxes from gaming in September. That brought the year-to-date total to $756.8 million. Total gaming revenue for the first nine months of 2026 reached $5.39 billion, 5.1% more than the $5.13 billion recorded over the same stretch of 2025. Sports betting slides as prediction markets draw scrutiny Sportsbooks had a much weaker month than online casinos. The calendar played a part, with one fewer football weekend this September. Tyrrell suggested that prediction markets such as Kalshi and Polymarket may be pulling bets away from regulated sportsbooks. He added that this could cut state tax revenue. The sports slowdown is not new. In August, the decline came mainly from a later start to some fall sports, including college football, compared with August 2025. A lower win percentage also weighed on that month. The split matters beyond New Jersey.
