The Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals ruled on August 28 that sports-related event contracts offered by prediction-market platforms are not swaps under federal commodities law, clearing the way for Nevada to apply its own gaming regulations to those products. The decision, detailed in ReadWrite’s earlier coverage of the case, denied requests for injunctive relief from Kalshi, Crypto.com, and Robinhood. All three platforms had asked the court to stop the Nevada Gaming Control Board from shutting down their sports-outcome contract offerings in the state. The court’s finding, according to Insider Monkey’s report via Yahoo Finance, was that these contracts function as sports bets rather than federally regulated swaps, putting them squarely within reach of state gambling law. The classification question sits at the center of a wider fight. Prediction-market operators and the CFTC have argued that event contracts, including sports-outcome products, qualify as swaps subject to exclusive federal jurisdiction, which would bar states from regulating or banning them outright. Forty-four states have pushed back, arguing the products are sports betting dressed up in derivatives language, a dispute tribal leaders have also raised with Congress. Why the Timing Matters for Sportsbooks Platforms like Kalshi have used the federal-swap designation to offer sports-outcome betting in states where traditional sportsbooks remain restricted or banned.
