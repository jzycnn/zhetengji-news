Churchill Downs Incorporated has asked the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) to order an independent review of the Horseracing Integrity and Safety Authority, escalating scrutiny of the regulator days before HISA provisionally suspended trainer Angel Quiroz over a series of alleged medication and horse-welfare violations. In an August 28 letter to FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson and Commissioner Mark Meador, Churchill Downs said a series of recent events had raised “significant questions about the governance, transparency, technology controls, and accountability” of HISA. The company asked the FTC, which oversees HISA, to require or initiate a formal independent review covering racing and wagering integrity, non-public information and the safeguarding of sensitive industry data. The intervention follows controversy over the so-called “Fair Hill Five” betting episode and HISA’s handling of confidential veterinary information accessed through its online portal by horse racing data analyst and owner Marshall Gramm. The latest enforcement action came on August 31, when HISA served Quiroz with a notice alleging violations of Rules 2215 and 2221. His registration was provisionally suspended from noon EDT on September 1, pending a provisional hearing. The notice says HISA believed there was “clear and convincing evidence” that his actions or inactions presented an immediate threat of serious injury or death to covered horses or riders. HISA alleges three horses under Quiroz’s care, Bonita Rough, Scootaloo and Tepeyac, returned adverse analytical findings for Albuterol, which is classified as a banned substance unless prescribed in specified circumstances. Each alleged anti-doping violation involving a banned substance carries a potential two-year period of ineligibility and $25,000 fine. Quiroz suspended after three horses test positive for Albuterol The first case involved Bonita Rough, whose blood sample was collected after a Vet’s List Workout at Fair Hill Training Center in Maryland on July 10. According to the notice, laboratory analysis detected Albuterol, while HISA Portal records showed no dispensation, prescription or treatment with the drug during the period Quiroz was the horse’s Responsible Person. HISA’s filing says Quiroz subsequently told investigators he had nebulised Bonita Rough with Albuterol obtained from the horse’s veterinarian at Gulfstream. “You stated that you nebulized Bonita Rough with Albuterol that you received from Bonita Rough’s Veterinarian at Gulfstream,” the charge letter quoted in the notice says. “You stated the Veterinarian had prescribed the medication to another horse but informed you that you could use it on Bonita Rough if needed.” HISA alleges that this amounted to administering a controlled medication outside a valid veterinarian-client-patient relationship. Its notice also says no corresponding Albuterol treatment records existed in the HISA Portal for Bonita Rough, Scootaloo or Tepeyac. The regulator said Quiroz was notified of the potential Bonita Rough violation on July 28. Thirteen days later, an out-of-competition sample was collected from Scootaloo at Saratoga Race Course and subsequently tested positive for Albuterol, according to the notice. A sample taken from Tepeyac at Fair Hill on August 13, 16 days after the initial notification concerning Bonita Rough, also returned an adverse analytical finding for Albuterol, HISA said. HISA said the alleged discovery of banned substances in multiple horses after Quiroz had already been notified of the first case posed an “immediate threat of serious injury or death to Covered Horses or Riders”. The allegations remain subject to the disciplinary process. Quiroz also faces a separate allegation that he caused Bonita Rough to breach her provisional suspension by posting a timed and reported workout at Fair Hill on July 29.
